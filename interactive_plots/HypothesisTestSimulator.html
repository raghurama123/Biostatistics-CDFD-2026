Hypothesis testing with a normal distribution

Suppose the test statistic follows a standard normal distribution when the null hypothesis is true:

$$ Z \sim N(0,1) $$

The null hypothesis represents no effect:

$$ H_0:\text{effect}=0 $$

For a two-tailed test:

$$ H_1:\text{effect}\ne0 $$

The basic decision rule is

$$ p \leq \alpha \quad\Rightarrow\quad \text{Reject }H_0 $$ $$ p > \alpha \quad\Rightarrow\quad \text{Do not reject }H_0 $$
Important: In a real hypothesis test, the p-value is calculated from the observed data. Here the p-value is changed manually so that you can explore how the decision depends on the p-value, significance level, and type of test.
Type I error: Rejecting $H_0$ when $H_0$ is actually true.

Type II error: Not rejecting $H_0$ when $H_1$ is actually true.

For a test performed at significance level $\alpha$, the probability of a Type I error is $\alpha$.
